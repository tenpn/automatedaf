Another GDC done - my 10th(!) in person. There were contrasts this time - the indies I'd never heard of who have found incredible success, the quietness of the gardens, how crowded the talks were over the first two days, the teeny tiny expo.

The badge changes that did away with the cheaper Expo-only pass but significantly lowered the cost of accessing the whole conference seem to at least have been a success. The automated testing roundtables I was moderating all week were very very well attended, and I heard similar things from other moderators.

If you came, thank you so much! I appreciate everyone who attends, contributes questions or experience.

The main theme coming away from the roundtables was that AI and LLMs are not having a huge impact yet. It took until Friday for the topic to even come up, and when it did it was in the context of people finding a few gains here and there, rather than upending their entire process. There seems to be a lot of scepticism especially around AI bot testing - although maybe as a group you'd expect us to take that position!

Having an agent do some exploratory or fuzz testing is one thing, but there's huge questions around accountability. If I ask an agent to test IAPs, they hallucinate or don't find all the purchases, then I ship a broken economy, who is accountable? It seems unavoidable that we'll always ask humans to test at least the golden path of the game, and maybe more.

But if agents can effectively interact with a game, are robust to changes, are affordable, and don't generate flaky results, they'll certainly find a place in the industry. Those are big Ifs though!

As always, happy testing.
