An argument I often hear against test automation in games is that it "takes too long".

Yes, there are literally more lines of code to write, since there are now tests in the project.

But you spend more time reading, extending and maintaining code than you ever will writing it out in the first place. That initial outlay of code, including tests or not, isn't really the bottleneck.

And we all intrinsically understand this, because we don't put Words Per Minute as a requirement in our job posts, and we stopped counting LOC as a useful metric decades ago!

Someone saying they don't have time for tests is the same as saying they don't have time for good architecture, and I think we all understand as an industry now what costs are coming for them down the road if they skimp on good foundations.

That team will also be spending time fixing bugs later either way - wouldn't they like those bugs to be fixed quickly and safely?

Plus, this whole thing has been studied. Here's one of many papers that compares a team doing Test Driven Development against a team not doing it, and found no reduction in productivity but an increase in the amount of defects found.

So next time someone tells you they don't have time for tests, ask them if they put WPM in their job adverts, and if not, why not.
