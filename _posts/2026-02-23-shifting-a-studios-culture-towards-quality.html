The Automated Testing In Games community (https://atig.dev) last week had a discussion about how to shift a game studio's culture towards more robust practices, like automated testing. Below is a summary of the takeaways:

David and Adam suggested letting actions speak louder than words: find a pain point, fix it, and let the team that you helped sing the praises of a quality-first approach.

Aäron's practical approach is to aim for simple high bang-for-buck changes to get things rolling, like a game boot smoke test that just checks the logs for errors. They also suggest talking directly to QA about what their current frustrations and time sinks are.

Jason had a long post with lots of useful suggestions that I will not plagiarise too much here (go and read the full thing on the discord) but I particularly liked his point about "automated tests" not always being the immediate answer. Something like a simple telemetry dashboard might be a huge game-changer, and open the door for further changes down the line.

My advice? Be vocal about the effects you are seeing from an under investment in quality - the cost to day-to-day productivity from undiscovered and unfixed bugs, high turnaround times, or how long QA's daily smoke actually is. Get people discussing these problems, and they'll soon come to you for answers.
