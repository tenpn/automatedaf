People are sometimes surprised when I say Automated AF works on both build engineering and test automation. Why not pick one and specialise? Won't that make it easier to get clients?

Well first of all I'm lucky enough that I don't need to worry about finding clients 😅 but really, this isn't two things.

What I really do is Quality Engineering for games studios, which manifests itself as mostly build servers and automated tests, but people aren't so familiar with that phrase, so let's talk about it now.

One surprising thing to note is that Quality Engineering is not strictly about shipping fewer bugs. It's interested in holistically asking, how do we ship better games for less time and money?

We understand that bugs that sit in a project cost money every day they're not fixed, and will be more expensive to fix the longer they live. So if you're interested in shipping better games for less time and money, finding and fixing bugs quickly can be a huge win. You'll be freeing staff of all disciplines to do deeper work more quickly.

This is where build engineering and test automation come in. It's one thing to have a CICD server that spits out a build quickly, and it's another to surface actionable errors quickly so people can jump on issues.

It's one thing to have good build engineering but have your test automation be flaky, badly-targeted or full of false positives.

And if the team aren't educated and don't have buy-in for this process, they will ignore the walls of red builds, charge head-first into new features, then wonder why the QA team is crying.

So I focus on that whole pipeline, from the moment someone commits code to source control to the moment a daily build is booted on a developer's machine. Joined-up thinking and a holistic view means both the build engineering and the test automation work benefit from each other, and are stronger than the sum of their parts.

And that's why what I do isn't two things, it's one thing: Quality Engineering.
