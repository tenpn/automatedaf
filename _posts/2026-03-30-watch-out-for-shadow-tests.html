--- layout: post title: "Watch Out For Shadow Tests" date: 2026-03-30 16:51:47 +0000 categories: [Test Automation] newsletter: true newsletter_id: 183348351975032742 ---

Last week’s post about how tests that don't fail don't really provide value. They don't get to do what every test lives to do: fail, and give you actionable instant feedback on a new defect.

Yet even though they're not really providing value, they have to be written, they have to be maintained, and they have to be executed.

There's another type of test that _does_ fail but provides no value when it does: the shadow test.

The shadow test is a test that only ever fails when another test fails. There could be a few reasons for this, but the most likely is that both tests are checking the same thing through different means.

I've seen this most commonly when people have a test fixture up and running, and add tests without plausibility, just "because they can" and "it can't hurt".

When you find one of these, it's worth investigating closely and making sure it's truly doing something independent and valuable. If not, it's time to kill that test - cut your losses and save your future maintenance time.

If you want to find more of these, then it can be really useful to have your tests hooked up to analytics. Then you can set up a search to cluster tests that always fail together, alongside other metrics like your most flaky tests, or the tests that have never ever failed.

Next time you're fixing some failures, ask yourself if one of these is a shadow test!

As always, happy testing.
