This week I want to encourage you to stop shaming build failures!

I used to work in a studio who had a plastic "fish of shame" that sat on the desk of whoever broke the build last. I assume they wanted people to do more local checks before pushing, but all they did was make people feel bad. I think our builds failed as much as anyone's.

The build server is there to fail, that's its purpose, that's why you made it. Sure you don't want anyone making repeated simple mistakes, but more often that not a failed build is evidence of time saved for everyone. As long as the team reacts quickly and learns from the failure, everything is working as intended.

That bug didn't have to go to QA

That bug didn't have to get logged

That bug didn't have to get triaged

That bug didn't have to ping around the houses until it found the best person to fix it

That bug didn't impact others while it lived in the build

And that bug didn't need the fixer to remember how the system worked to fix it correctly.

Let's stop shaming and start celebrating how much time build server failures are saving us.
